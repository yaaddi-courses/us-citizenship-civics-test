# Congress and its two houses

**Covers:** Congress, Senate, House of Representatives, Great Compromise, Congressional district, Speaker of the House

## One legislature with two halves {#overview}
Why does the United States have two chambers that make laws, when one might seem enough? The answer comes from a bitter argument in 1787 between large states and small ones. A bill must pass both chambers before it can reach the president. Once you understand the compromise, you will see why the two chambers look so different.

## Congress {#congress}
Congress is the legislature of the United States. It writes federal laws, sets taxes and spending, and can declare war. It meets in the Capitol building in Washington, D.C. A new Congress starts every two years and is numbered, so the one that began in January 2025 is the 119th.

## Senate {#senate}
The Senate has 100 members, two from each state, no matter how big or small the state is. A senator serves a six-year term, and about one-third of the seats are up for election every two years. To be a senator you must be at least 30 years old, a citizen for at least nine years, and live in the state you represent. The vice president presides over the Senate and votes only to break a tie.

## House of Representatives {#house-of-representatives}
The House of Representatives works differently. It has 435 voting members, and the number each state gets depends on its population. A representative serves a two-year term, so the whole House is elected every two years. A representative must be at least 25 years old and a citizen for at least seven years. The House also has a few delegates from places like Washington, D.C. and Puerto Rico, who can take part in debates but cannot vote on final passage.

## Great Compromise {#great-compromise}
Here is how the two chambers came about. At the Constitutional Convention in 1787, large states wanted representation by population. Small states wanted every state to count equally. Neither side would give way, so the delegates combined both ideas. Each state got two senators, and House seats were set by population. This deal is the Great Compromise. It is also called the Connecticut Compromise, after the state of its main author, Roger Sherman.

## Congressional district {#congressional-district}
Inside a state, the House seats are tied to places. A congressional district is a section of a state whose voters elect one member of the House. After each census, held every ten years, seats are reassigned among the states, and states redraw their district lines so that each one has roughly the same number of people. With about 331 million people in the 2020 census, each district holds on average about 760,000 residents.

## Speaker of the House {#speaker-of-the-house}
The Speaker of the House leads the House of Representatives. The members elect the Speaker, usually from the largest party. The Speaker decides much of what the House debates and, after the vice president, is next in line for the presidency.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's compare two states in the 2020 count.

| | California | Wyoming |
|---|---|---|
| Population | about 39.5 million | about 0.58 million |
| House seats | 52 | 1 |
| Senate seats | 2 | 2 |
| Total in Congress | 54 | 3 |

Divide 39.5 by 0.58 and you find that California has about 68 times the population of Wyoming. In the House, California has 52 times the seats, which follows population closely. In the Senate, the two states are equal. Both houses must agree on every law, so a big state cannot ignore small ones, and small states cannot outvote big ones on their own. That balance is the point of the Great Compromise.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is mixing up the numbers: 100 senators with six-year terms, and 435 representatives with two-year terms. A memory aid is that the smaller house has the longer term.

A second slip is thinking that Congress means only the House. It means both chambers together.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Congress has two houses: the Senate (100 members, six-year terms) and the House of Representatives (435 voting members, two-year terms).
- The Great Compromise gave each state two senators and set House seats by population.
- Each congressional district elects one representative.
- The Speaker of the House leads the House and is next in line after the vice president.
