# The Constitution and the idea of self-government

**Covers:** Republic, Representative democracy, Constitution, Consent of the governed, Amendment, Bill of Rights

## A government with written rules {#overview}
Every country has some way of deciding who makes the rules. The United States chose a particular answer: the people hold power, they choose representatives to use it, and a written document says what those representatives may and may not do. This article follows that chain of ideas from the broad idea to the document and then to how it can change.

## Republic {#republic}
A republic is a country in which power comes from the people and is used by leaders they elect, under a set of rules. It is the opposite of a monarchy, where the right to rule passes through a family. The United States has been a republic since its founding.

In a republic, no leader holds power for life just by being born into it. Presidents, senators and representatives are elected, and they serve for limited terms.

## Representative democracy {#representative-democracy}
In a representative democracy, citizens vote for people who make laws on their behalf, not on every single law. It is called indirect democracy for that reason. A direct democracy would ask all the voters to decide each issue, which is hard in a country of more than 330 million people.

The United States is both a republic and a representative democracy. The two words describe the same system from two angles. Republic points to elected leaders and written rules. Representative democracy points to the way the voters choose lawmakers.

## Constitution {#constitution}
The Constitution is the written set of basic rules that sets up the government, limits its powers and protects people's rights. It was written in 1787 and has been in effect since 1789. It is the highest law of the land, which means that no law may contradict it.

It has seven main articles. The first three describe the three branches of government. The others deal with the states, with changing the Constitution, with the supremacy of federal law and with how it was approved. It is short compared with many national constitutions, which is part of why courts and citizens still debate its meaning.

## Consent of the governed {#consent-of-the-governed}
The consent of the governed is the idea that a government has the right to rule only because the people agree to be ruled. The founders stated it in 1776, when they broke away from British rule. Voting and peaceful elections are the main ways people give or withdraw that consent.

It also explains why free elections matter. If the voters cannot remove leaders, the government no longer rests on their agreement.

## Amendment {#amendment}
An amendment is an official change or addition to the text of the Constitution. The founders knew the document would need changes, so they included a method. Two-thirds of both houses of the national legislature propose an amendment, and then three-quarters of the states, which means 38 of 50, must approve it.

The bar is high on purpose. Only 27 amendments have been adopted since 1789, so the Constitution changes slowly and only with broad agreement.

## Bill of Rights {#bill-of-rights}
The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments, added in 1791. They protect basic freedoms against the government. The First Amendment protects freedom of religion, speech, the press, assembly and petition. Other amendments protect people from unreasonable searches, guarantee a fair trial, and keep powers not given to the federal government for the states and the people.

Some states would only approve the Constitution if rights were added, which is why they came right after.

## A worked example {#example}
How many states must approve an amendment? Use the figures above.

1. Total states: 50.
2. Required share: three-quarters, which is 0.75.
3. 50 times 0.75 equals 37.5.
4. States cannot be split, so round up to 38.

How many can block it? If 13 states say no, only 37 remain, and 37 is less than 38, so the amendment fails. A little more than a quarter of the states can stop any change.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Using "democracy" and "republic" as opposites. The United States is both. Another mistake is believing the Bill of Rights is separate from the Constitution: it is part of it, as the first ten amendments.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The United States is a republic and a representative democracy: leaders are elected under written rules.
- The Constitution is the supreme law, written in 1787, and can be changed only by amendment.
- An amendment needs two-thirds of both houses of the legislature and 38 of 50 states.
- The Bill of Rights, the first ten amendments, protects basic freedoms.
- The consent of the governed means the government rules because the people agree.
