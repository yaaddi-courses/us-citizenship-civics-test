# The ideas behind the American system

**Covers:** Declaration of Independence, Natural rights, Capitalism, Rule of law

## Four ideas that shaped a country {#overview}
Many of the questions on the civics test are about facts: a date, a name, a number. Facts are easier to remember when you know the ideas behind them. Four ideas appear again and again. People have rights from birth. A free country announced itself in a famous document. The economy should be mostly free. And nobody stands above the law. Let's take them one at a time.

## Declaration of Independence {#declaration-of-independence}
On 4 July 1776, delegates from the colonies adopted a document announcing that the thirteen American colonies were free from British rule. That document is the Declaration of Independence. Thomas Jefferson wrote most of it, and that is why 4 July is celebrated as Independence Day.

It has two main parts. The first explains why governments exist and when people may replace them. The second lists complaints against the British king, such as taxing the colonies without their consent and keeping soldiers among the people in peacetime. It ends by declaring independence.

## Natural rights {#natural-rights}
Where do rights come from? The founders answered that people have basic rights only because they are human, and not because a government granted them. These are natural rights. The Declaration names three: life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness. The English thinker John Locke influenced this idea. He argued that governments are formed to protect such rights, and that a government which fails to do so loses its authority.

The same thought lies behind the later Bill of Rights, which lists protections that the government must respect.

## Capitalism {#capitalism}
Now to the economy. Under capitalism, private people and businesses own most property and compete in a free market. Prices are set mainly by what buyers and sellers agree to, not by the state. Anyone may start a business, hire workers and keep the profit.

The United States has a market economy, but not a pure one. The government also sets rules, such as safety standards and minimum wages, collects taxes and provides some services. Economists call that a mixed economy.

## Rule of law {#rule-of-law}
Under the rule of law, everyone must obey the law, including the people who make and enforce it. No one gets special treatment because of wealth, office or fame. A mayor who speeds gets a ticket like anyone else, and a president must follow the Constitution.

It also means that laws must be public and applied fairly, and that disputes are settled by courts, not by force. Without it, the other ideas would be only words.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's match each famous line from the Declaration to the idea behind it.

| Line | Idea |
|---|---|
| "All men are created equal" | Equality before the law |
| "Endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable rights... Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness" | Natural rights |
| "...deriving their powers from the consent of the governed" | Consent of the governed |
| "It is the Right of the People to alter or to abolish it" | The people may replace a government that fails |

Notice that none of these lines mentions money or markets. Capitalism comes from a separate tradition, in particular from Adam Smith's book of 1776, published in the same year as the Declaration. The overlap is an interesting coincidence, but the two ideas are distinct.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is thinking that the rights in the Declaration are legal rules you can enforce in court. It is a statement of principles. The enforceable rules came later, in the Constitution and its amendments.

A second slip is believing that capitalism means no government role at all.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The Declaration of Independence, adopted 4 July 1776, announced freedom from British rule.
- Natural rights, such as life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, are held by people from birth.
- Capitalism means private ownership and a free market, with some government rules.
- Under the rule of law, everyone, including leaders, must obey the law.
